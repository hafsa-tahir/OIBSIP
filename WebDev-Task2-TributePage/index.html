NIKOLA TESLA

1856 – 1943 · Engineer of alternating current, wireless transmission, and the electrified century that followed him.

FIG. 01 01 · SMILJAN, CROATIA

A Mind Wired for Invention

Born in 1856 in the village of Smiljan, Tesla showed an early gift for visualizing entire machines in his head before ever touching a tool. He claimed he could build and test an invention mentally, down to its wear points, without drawing a single line.

He studied engineering in Graz and Prague, and it was there, staring at a sparking DC motor, that he first imagined a rotating magnetic field — the idea that alternating current could spin in continuous, elegant motion.

FIG. 02 02 · THE CURRENT WAR

Alternating Current, Set Loose

In 1884 Tesla arrived in New York with little more than a letter of introduction to Thomas Edison. Their partnership was brief; Tesla's belief in alternating current put him at odds with Edison's DC empire. By 1888 his AC induction motor patents had been licensed by George Westinghouse.

The rivalry became public spectacle. Tesla's system won the contract to light the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, and two years later it was harnessing Niagara Falls itself — proving power could travel vast distances.

FIG. 03 03 · WARDENCLYFFE, LONG ISLAND

A World Without Wires

Tesla's ambitions outgrew the power grid. In 1898 he demonstrated a radio-controlled boat to a startled New York audience — one of the first working precursors of robotics. He believed the same principle could transmit electricity through the air.

Construction began on the Wardenclyffe Tower in 1901, meant to broadcast wireless power across the Atlantic. Funding collapsed before full testing, but its silhouette remains a towering symbol of visionary foresight.

FIG. 04 04 · LEGACY

What Still Runs on His Ideas

Tesla died in New York in 1943, largely out of the public eye. Yet the alternating current system he championed became the backbone of the electrical grid the world still runs on — from household appliances to massive city substations.

His fingerprints span radio, remote control, and wireless theory. Modern digital and electrical infrastructure trace a direct line back to the visionary patents he filed over a century ago.
