01 INTRODUCTION

Marie Curie transformed the world of science through her relentless curiosity and groundbreaking research into radioactivity.

Her work challenged scientific assumptions of her era and opened new paths in physics, chemistry, and medicine. She became one of the most influential scientists in modern history.

02 / BIOGRAPHY

01

Early Years

Born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw in 1867, Curie grew up in a family that valued education. Opportunities for women in higher education were severely limited in her homeland, but her determination to study never faded.

02

Paris & Science

She eventually moved to Paris and enrolled at the Sorbonne, where she studied physics and mathematics. Despite financial difficulties and demanding living conditions, she excelled academically and built the foundation for her scientific career.

03

Groundbreaking Research

Working alongside Pierre Curie, she investigated mysterious emissions produced by certain materials. Their research led to the identification of new elements, including polonium and radium, and helped establish radioactivity as an important scientific field.

04

A Lasting Legacy

Curie's scientific achievements earned her two Nobel Prizes in different scientific disciplines. During the First World War, she also supported the use of mobile X-ray units to help doctors treat wounded soldiers. Her discoveries continued to influence medicine and scientific research long after her death.

03 / TIMELINE

1867
WARSAW

A Scientist Is Born

Maria Skłodowska was born in Warsaw, then part of the Russian Empire. Her family encouraged education despite difficult political and economic circumstances.

1891
PARIS

A New Academic Life

Curie moved to Paris and began studying at the University of Paris, where she pursued physics and mathematics.

1898
DISCOVERY

Polonium & Radium

Marie and Pierre Curie announced the discovery of polonium and later identified radium while studying radioactive materials.

1903
NOBEL PRIZE

Nobel Prize in Physics

Curie shared the Nobel Prize in Physics with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel for their contributions to understanding radiation.

1911
NOBEL PRIZE

Nobel Prize in Chemistry

She received a second Nobel Prize, this time in Chemistry, for her work involving radium and polonium and the study of their properties.

1914–18
WORLD WAR I

Science on the Front Lines

Curie helped develop and organize mobile radiography units that allowed X-ray examinations to be carried out closer to wounded soldiers.

04 / LEGACY

Marie Curie's influence reaches far beyond the discoveries that earned her international recognition.

Her research helped establish radioactivity as a major area of scientific investigation and contributed to later developments in medical diagnosis and treatment.

Just as important was the example she set. Curie's career demonstrated how persistence, intellectual curiosity, and rigorous research could overcome barriers that once seemed impossible.
